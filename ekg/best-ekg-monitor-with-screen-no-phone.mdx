The best EKG monitor with a built-in screen is one that shows your result on the device itself in about 30 seconds — no phone to unlock, no app to open, and no subscription. The SonoHealth EKGraph does exactly this: an FDA-cleared single-lead ECG with its own LCD that classifies each trace on the spot. It screens and tracks your heart rhythm; it does not diagnose.

<Warning>
A personal EKG cannot rule out a heart attack. If you have chest pain, fainting, or severe shortness of breath, **call 911** — do not stop to record a trace.
</Warning>

## Why does a built-in screen matter?

Palpitations are often brief, so the moment they hit is when you want to record — and fumbling to unlock a phone and open an app can cost you the reading. A monitor with its own screen lets you touch the sensors and see a classification immediately, which many people find simpler, especially [seniors](/ekg/best-personal-ekg-for-seniors) or anyone who isn't phone-first. See [how a home EKG works](/ekg/overview).

## Screen-based vs. app-only EKGs

Many personal EKGs are **app-first**: the device is a sensor and your phone is the screen. That works, but it adds a step and sometimes a paid membership for storage. A **screen-based** device is self-contained — useful when you want to hand it to a spouse or parent, or record without your phone nearby. Compare the two approaches in [EKGraph vs. KardiaMobile](/ekg/ekgraph-vs-kardiamobile) and [personal EKG vs. smartwatch](/ekg/personal-ekg-vs-smartwatch).
