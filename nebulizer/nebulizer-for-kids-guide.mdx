Giving a young child a breathing treatment can feel daunting, but with the right setup and a calm routine it becomes manageable. This guide covers practical tips for using a portable nebulizer with children.

<Note>
Use only medications or saline that your child's pediatrician has prescribed or approved. For infants, ask your pediatrician before using anything in a nebulizer, including sterile saline, and follow their instructions on what and how much to use.
</Note>

## Mask or mouthpiece — which should my child use?

Younger children do best with a well-fitting **mask** held gently over the nose and mouth, while older children who can seal their lips can use the **mouthpiece** for more efficient delivery. The [MistPro](/nebulizer/overview) comes with an adult mask and a child mask, and the unit itself has a mouthpiece, so you can match the device to your child's age as they grow.

## Does the "blow-by" method work?

Holding the mask snugly against the face is far more effective than "blow-by" — waving mist near the face — because gaps let medication escape. A snug, comfortable mask with no gaps delivers the most medication to the lungs. The MistPro is quiet (50 dB or less, per its manual), with no loud compressor to frighten a child.

## How do I keep a restless toddler calm?

- Treat during a calm moment, such as during a favorite show or story.
- Let the child hold the small, quiet device or decorate the case to feel in control.
- Keep treatments short. The MistPro manual says each session should be no longer than 20 minutes, and a guardian must be present when a child uses it.
- Stay relaxed yourself; children take cues from caregivers.

## Can I use it for RSV, croup, or congestion?

Your pediatrician may suggest nebulized **sterile saline** during some illnesses. Ask them first: saline is sold without a prescription, but whether to use it, how much and how often is your pediatrician's call. See [saline explained](/nebulizer/saline-explained). Any prescription medication (such as a bronchodilator) must come from your pediatrician. Suspensions such as budesonide are not for the MistPro, per its manual.

## How important is cleaning between uses?

Very. Children are vulnerable to infection, and the MistPro manual says to clean and disinfect the device after every use with 75% ethanol, then rinse well and dry. Follow each step in the [cleaning guide](/nebulizer/cleaning-guide), and never touch the mesh. If your child's doctor prescribes an inhaled steroid, it will usually be a suspension that needs a different nebulizer; follow the instructions that come with it.

<Warning>
Seek urgent care if your child has severe difficulty breathing, fast breathing with chest pulling-in (retractions), blue lips, won't drink, or is unusually drowsy. A nebulizer does not replace emergency care, and a rescue treatment that isn't working is an emergency.
</Warning>
