## How Nebulizers Are Regulated

In the US, nebulizers are **Class II medical devices** (21 CFR 868.5630), and a new nebulizer generally reaches the market through the FDA's 510(k) **clearance** process. "Approved" is the word for higher-risk Class III devices that go through premarket approval. The MistPro manual says the product "should be purchased and used under the guidance of a doctor."

A clearance number for the MistPro is not published on this page yet. You can check any nebulizer in the FDA database (see below).

## Why FDA Status Matters for Nebulizers

When a nebulizer delivers prescribed medicine, for example ipratropium bromide solution, the device is part of how the medicine reaches your lungs. The device's particle size, output and safety affect:

- Whether the right dose reaches the lungs
- Whether contamination can happen
- Whether the device performs the same way over time

A cleared nebulizer has been reviewed for:
- **Electrical safety** (IEC 60601 standard for medical electrical equipment)
- **Biocompatibility** of parts that touch the patient
- **Performance**, such as particle size and nebulization rate
- **Labeling** that matches its tested performance

## How to Check Any Nebulizer's FDA Status

FDA clearance records are public. Search the [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm) by manufacturer or device name. Nebulizers use product code **CAF**. Many imported mesh nebulizers sold on Amazon and other retail channels show no clearance number, so check before you buy, especially if you'll use it with prescribed medicine.

## What the MistPro Manual Covers

- Particle size: median 1–4 microns (±25%), with at least 60% of the mist, by volume, under 5 microns
- Mist rate: at least 0.25 ml/min (setting I) and 0.15 ml/min (setting II)
- Medicine: follow your doctor's advice; no suspensions or high-concentration, high-viscosity, oily or volatile medicines
- Cleaning: disinfect after every use with 75% ethanol

<Note>
The MistPro does not include medicine. Most nebulizer medicines need a prescription from your healthcare provider. Saline solution for inhalation is sold over the counter, for example on Amazon. See [Which medicines work](/nebulizer/medications-compatible).
</Note>
