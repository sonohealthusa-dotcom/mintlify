If you or a family member needs breathing treatments, a portable nebulizer can make therapy quieter and possible almost anywhere. But devices vary widely. This guide covers the features that matter most so you can choose well.

<Note>
A nebulizer is a drug-delivery device. It does not include medicine, and most breathing medicines need a prescription. Use only medicines your doctor prescribes or approves, and follow their instructions.
</Note>

## Mesh, jet, or ultrasonic — which technology is best?

The three main types differ in how they turn liquid into mist. **Vibrating mesh** nebulizers like the [MistPro](/nebulizer/how-it-works) push medicine through a fine mesh; they're quiet, hand-sized and battery-powered. **Jet (compressor)** nebulizers are larger and louder and usually need a wall outlet, but many can handle suspension medicines. **Ultrasonic** units can warm the medicine, which may affect some drugs. Our [mesh vs jet](/nebulizer/mesh-vs-jet) page compares them in depth.

## Which medicines will you use?

Check first. The MistPro manual says not to use suspended, high-concentration, high-viscosity, oily or volatile medicines. That rules out suspensions such as budesonide (Pulmicort Respules). If your medicine is a suspension, ask your doctor which nebulizer type fits. See [Which medicines work](/nebulizer/medications-compatible).

## Why does particle size matter?

Particles of about **1 to 5 microns** reach the smaller airways. The MistPro manual says at least 60% of its particles are under 5 microns. See [how it works](/nebulizer/how-it-works).

## How quiet should it be?

Noise matters for daily use and for children. The MistPro manual lists noise at 50 dB or less, much quieter than a typical jet compressor. Treatment time depends on the dose and mist setting. Keep each session to 20 minutes or less.

## What about battery, portability, and what's included?

Look for a rechargeable battery so you can treat in bed, in the car, or while [traveling](/nebulizer/travel-use). The MistPro runs about 1 hour on a full charge. Check what comes in the box. The MistPro includes the device, medicine cup, an adult mask, a child mask, a power cord and the manual.

## Is FDA status important?

Yes. Nebulizers are Class II medical devices in the US and are cleared through the FDA 510(k) process. Check any device in the [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm). See [MistPro FDA status](/nebulizer/fda-approval).

## Buyer's checklist

- Takes the type of medicine you use (solution vs suspension)
- Vibrating mesh technology (quiet, portable)
- Particles mostly under 5 microns
- Rechargeable battery
- Adult and child masks included
- Clear FDA status and easy-to-follow [cleaning](/nebulizer/cleaning-guide) instructions

<Warning>
A nebulizer is not a rescue device for a severe attack on its own, and it cannot replace emergency care. If breathing is severe, lips or face look blue, or a rescue inhaler isn't helping, call emergency services. See [when to seek urgent care](/nebulizer/asthma-relief).
</Warning>
