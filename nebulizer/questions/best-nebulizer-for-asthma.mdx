## What Makes a Nebulizer Good for Asthma

Asthma treatment with a nebulizer delivers prescribed medicine as a fine mist directly into the airways. In general, particles of about **1–5 microns** reach the airways best.

The MistPro manual lists a median particle size of 1–4 µm (±25%), with at least 60% of the mist, by volume, in particles under 5 µm.

## Why Mesh Can Suit Home Asthma Care

**Treatment time depends on your dose.** The MistPro manual lists a mist rate of at least 0.25 ml per minute on setting I and at least 0.15 ml per minute on setting II, and a maximum of 20 minutes per session. Follow the treatment time your doctor gives you.

**Portability matters for asthma management.** Asthma symptoms can occur anywhere. A pocket-sized, battery-powered nebulizer can be used at home, at work, in the car, or while traveling — without needing an outlet.

**Noise matters for children and nighttime use.** Jet nebulizers are loud, which can distress children and disrupt sleep. The MistPro manual lists a noise level of 50 dB or less.

## Which Asthma Medicines Can Go in the MistPro?

The MistPro works with **solutions**, not suspensions. Its manual says commonly nebulized medicines include bronchodilators, moistening expectorants and antibiotics, "such as terbutaline sulphate solution, ipratropium bromide solution." It also says: "Do not use suspended or high-concentration medicinal solutions."

- **Suspensions such as budesonide (Pulmicort Respules) should not be used in the MistPro.** If your prescription is a suspension, ask your doctor or pharmacist which nebulizer fits it.
- For any other prescribed medicine, ask your pharmacist: "Is this a solution or a suspension?" Your doctor decides the medicine, dose and treatment time.
- See [Which medicines work in the MistPro](/nebulizer/medications-compatible) for the full list from the manual.

<Note>
Always use physician-prescribed or physician-approved medications. Do not use oil-based substances, essential oils, or undiluted herbal preparations in a nebulizer.
</Note>

## Who Should Use the MistPro for Asthma

**Adults with mild to moderate asthma** who need fast, convenient bronchodilator delivery at home or on the go.

**Children with asthma** — the MistPro includes a child mask and is quiet (50 dB or less, per the manual), which can make it easier to use with young patients than a loud compressor. The manual says a guardian must be present when a child uses it.

**People with exercise-induced asthma** who need portable relief.

**Frequent travelers** who can't carry a bulky compressor nebulizer.

## What to Expect During Treatment

1. Fill the medication cup with your prescribed nebulizer solution
2. Attach the appropriate mask or mouthpiece
3. Press the single button to start
4. Breathe normally through the mask or mouthpiece for the time your doctor prescribes (the manual says no more than 20 minutes per session)
5. Clean and disinfect the cup after each use as the manual describes (don't touch the mesh)

## When to Seek Emergency Care

<Warning>
A home nebulizer is for managing asthma symptoms as directed by your physician — not for managing asthma emergencies. If your symptoms are severe, worsening, or not responding to your prescribed treatment, seek emergency medical care immediately.
</Warning>

Signs you need emergency care:
- Difficulty speaking in full sentences
- Lips or fingernails turning blue
- Chest retractions (skin pulling in at the ribs)
- No improvement after prescribed rescue medication dose
