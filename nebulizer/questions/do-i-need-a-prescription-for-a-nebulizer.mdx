In the US, nebulizers are Class II medical devices, and most home nebulizers are cleared for use on a doctor's order. Most of the medicines you put in a nebulizer need a prescription too. The MistPro manual says the product "should be purchased and used under the guidance of a doctor." So before you buy, talk to your doctor about your medicine and the right device for it.

<Warning>
  A nebulizer is not a substitute for emergency care. If breathing is severe, lips or face look blue, or a rescue inhaler isn't helping, call 911.
</Warning>

## Why do nebulizers need a doctor's guidance?

A nebulizer turns liquid medicine into a mist you breathe in. The device is only as safe as the medicine and the dose that go into it. Most nebulizer medicines, such as bronchodilators, are prescription drugs. Your doctor decides the medicine, the dose, and how often you use it. The MistPro manual says: "Please follow doctors' advice about medication species, dosage and usage."

## Do I need a prescription for the medicine?

For most nebulizer medicines, yes. Sterile saline for inhalation is sold without a prescription, but ask your clinician whether saline is right for you and how much to use, especially for a child.

## Which medicines work in the MistPro SN-07?

- The manual names solutions such as **terbutaline sulphate** and **ipratropium bromide** as commonly used.
- The manual says: "Do not use suspended or high-concentration medicinal solutions." That means **budesonide suspension (Pulmicort Respules) is not for the MistPro.**
- Don't use high-viscosity, oily or volatile medicines, water, or essential oils.

If your medicine is a suspension, ask your doctor which nebulizer fits it. See [Which medicines work](/nebulizer/medications-compatible).

## What to ask your doctor

1. Which medicine and dose should I use in a nebulizer?
2. Is my medicine a solution or a suspension?
3. How often, and for how long, should each treatment be? (The MistPro manual says each session should be no longer than 20 minutes.)
4. For a child: which medicine and amount? (The manual says a guardian must be present.)
