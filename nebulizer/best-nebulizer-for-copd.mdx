For many people managing COPD at home, a portable vibrating mesh nebulizer like the SonoHealth MistPro is a good fit. It's quiet (50 dB or less, per its manual), runs on a rechargeable battery, and works with prescribed **solution** medicines such as ipratropium bromide. It's easier for daily use than a bulky, loud jet nebulizer. If your medicine is a suspension, ask your doctor which device fits it.

<Note>
A nebulizer delivers medicine your doctor prescribes. It is not a cure for COPD and does not replace your prescribed inhalers, oxygen, or action plan. Always follow your pulmonologist's or clinician's instructions.
</Note>

## What is the best nebulizer for COPD at home?

For most people using solution medicines at home, a mesh nebulizer is convenient. The [MistPro](/nebulizer/overview) is hand-sized and quiet, so daily maintenance treatments are unobtrusive, even at night. Its manual says at least 60% of its mist, by volume, is in particles under 5 microns (median 1–4 microns), a size suited to the smaller airways. The rechargeable battery runs about 1 hour per charge, so you aren't tied to an outlet.

## What features matter most for COPD?

When choosing a nebulizer for COPD, prioritize:

- **The right type for your medicine**: mesh units like the MistPro take solutions, not suspensions
- **Clear FDA status**: nebulizers are Class II devices cleared through the FDA 510(k) process
- **Fine particle size** for deep-airway deposition
- **Quiet treatments** to make daily therapy sustainable
- **Rechargeable, portable design** for treatments at home, in the car or while traveling
- **Easy cleaning** to reduce the infection risk that can trigger a flare
- **A real warranty and return policy**: the MistPro includes a 2-year warranty and 60-day money-back guarantee

## Which medications are used for COPD in a nebulizer?

Doctors commonly prescribe nebulized bronchodilators, such as ipratropium, for COPD, and sometimes inhaled corticosteroids like budesonide. The MistPro manual names solutions such as ipratropium bromide and terbutaline sulphate, and says not to use suspensions, so **budesonide suspension is not for the MistPro**. See [Which medicines work](/nebulizer/medications-compatible). Never nebulize oil-based substances or essential oils, and confirm your exact regimen with your clinician.

## How often can a COPD patient use a nebulizer?

Use your nebulizer exactly as prescribed. Many COPD patients use maintenance bronchodilators on a schedule, plus a rescue treatment as directed. Don't exceed the frequency your doctor set. If you need rescue treatments more often, that can signal a flare, so contact your clinician. Keep each MistPro session to 20 minutes or less, per the manual. See [COPD management](/nebulizer/copd-management).

## How do I keep treatments hygienic to avoid infections?

Respiratory infections can trigger COPD flares, so clean and disinfect the MistPro after every use: 75% ethanol for the medicine cup and accessories, then rinse well with clean water and dry, as the manual describes. Don't share accessories between people. The [cleaning guide](/nebulizer/cleaning-guide) has every step.

## When should COPD symptoms send me to urgent care?

Home nebulizer therapy manages day-to-day symptoms, but it does not replace emergency care.

<Warning>
Seek urgent help if you have severe shortness of breath, your rescue medication is not working, your lips or fingertips look blue, you are confused or very drowsy, or your oxygen level drops. Call emergency services for a severe COPD exacerbation.
</Warning>
