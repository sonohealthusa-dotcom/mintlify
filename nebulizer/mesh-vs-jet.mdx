The two main types of home nebulizers are **mesh nebulizers** (like the MistPro) and **jet nebulizers** (also called compressor nebulizers). For many patients using solution medicines at home, a mesh nebulizer is the more convenient choice. Ask your doctor which type fits your medicine.

## Noise: The Most Common Complaint About Jet Nebulizers

Jet nebulizers use an electric air compressor, which makes noticeable noise. Common complaints:

- Hard to use at night without waking others
- Children resist the loud sound
- Hard to use discreetly at work or in public

The MistPro manual lists noise at 50 dB or less. That helps with:
- **Children** (with a guardian present, per the manual)
- **Night-time treatments**
- **Work or travel use**

## Treatment Time

Treatment time depends on how much medicine is in the cup and the mist setting. The MistPro manual lists mist rates of at least 0.25 ml/min (setting I) and 0.15 ml/min (setting II), and says each session should last no longer than 20 minutes.

## Portability

A typical jet nebulizer includes:
- A compressor unit (tabletop size)
- Tubing
- A nebulizer cup
- A mouthpiece or mask

Most jet nebulizer users only use them at home, plugged into a wall. The MistPro fits in your hand and runs on a rechargeable battery.

## When Jet Nebulizers Might Still Be Appropriate

- **Suspension medicines** (such as budesonide), which the MistPro manual says not to use
- **Hospital/clinical settings** with heavy use
- **When an outlet is always available** and portability doesn't matter
