Recent research has detected micro- and nanoplastic particles in human brain tissue, and one 2024–2025 analysis reported that concentrations appeared to rise between 2016 and 2024. That sounds alarming, but the science is young: finding particles is **not** the same as proving they cause harm, and no supplement is shown to remove them. The evidence-based response is to lower everyday exposure, not to panic or chase "detox" cures.

<Note>
This is an educational overview, not medical advice. Micro- and nanoplastics are an emerging research area with real uncertainty. If you have specific health concerns, talk with your clinician rather than relying on any product or at-home "toxin" test.
</Note>

## Have microplastics really been found in the brain?

Yes. Using advanced lab methods, researchers have identified micro- and nanoplastic particles in human organs including the liver, kidney, and brain, with some studies reporting **higher concentrations in brain tissue** than in liver or kidney, largely as tiny polyethylene fragments. Particles have also been reported in blood, placenta, and other tissues.

What this doesn't tell us yet: whether those particles *cause* disease, what level (if any) is harmful, or how much comes from food, water, or air. Much of the "effects" evidence so far is from lab and animal models, not proof of harm in people.

## Why nanoplastics get special attention

Nanoplastics are far smaller than microplastics (under about 1 micron). Because of their size, researchers think they may be more able to cross biological barriers and enter cells and tissues. That's a plausible concern worth studying — but "plausible" is not "proven," and safe-vs-unsafe thresholds haven't been established. See [are microplastics harmful?](/plastiq-off/are-microplastics-harmful) for the fuller picture.

## Can you "detox" microplastics from your brain?

No — and be skeptical of anything that claims to. A swallowed binder acts only in your gut; it cannot reach or "pull" particles out of your brain or bloodstream. What the evidence actually supports is **reducing intake** and **supporting your body's normal elimination**:

- Swap plastic for **glass or stainless steel**, especially for hot food and drinks.
- Don't microwave food in plastic; avoid plastic [tea bags](/plastiq-off/microplastics-in-food).
- **Filter your tap water** (quality carbon-block or reverse osmosis).
- Eat **fiber**, stay **hydrated**, and ventilate/vacuum to cut household dust.

See [how to reduce microplastics in your body](/plastiq-off/how-to-reduce-microplastics) and [in your drinking water](/plastiq-off/reduce-microplastics-in-drinking-water) for specifics.

## Common questions

**Should I be scared that microplastics are in the brain?** Concerned enough to reduce exposure — yes. Panicked — no. Detection is real, but proof of harm and safe thresholds are still being worked out.

**Do microplastics cross the placenta?** Studies have reported micro- and nanoplastics in placental tissue. What it means for pregnancy outcomes isn't established; reducing exposure is the sensible precaution, and pregnant people should follow their clinician's guidance.

**Is there a test for microplastics in my body?** Not for everyday clinical use — measurement currently requires specialized research methods. Skip direct-to-consumer "toxin" tests.
