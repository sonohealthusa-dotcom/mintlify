Most of the microplastics people swallow pass through the **gut**, and early research — mostly in animals and lab models — suggests microplastics *may* disturb the gut lining and microbiome balance, but strong human evidence is still lacking. The best-supported, low-risk steps are the practical ones: reduce plastic exposure, eat more fiber, and stay hydrated to support your body's natural elimination.

## How microplastics reach the gut

The gut is the main route of exposure because microplastics turn up in food and drinks — bottled and tap water, seafood, salt, and foods packaged or heated in plastic. From there, most particles are passed in stool, while a small fraction may cross the gut lining; where those go and whether they cause harm is still being studied. See [how microplastics get into the body](/plastiq-off/microplastics-in-food).

## What early research suggests

- **Gut barrier:** Animal and cell studies suggest high microplastic exposure may stress the intestinal lining, but human relevance is unproven.
- **Microbiome:** Some models show shifts in gut bacteria; whether everyday human exposure changes the microbiome meaningfully isn't established.
- **Inflammation:** Lab work raises questions about low-grade inflammation, but this hasn't been demonstrated to cause disease in people.

The honest summary: interesting early signals, not settled conclusions. See [are microplastics harmful?](/plastiq-off/are-microplastics-harmful) for the broader evidence picture.

## Well-supported steps that actually help

1. **Cut plastic contact with food and drink** — favor glass or stainless steel, and don't microwave food in plastic. See [how to reduce microplastics](/plastiq-off/how-to-reduce-microplastics).
2. **Filter your drinking water** and consider that boiling hard water can reduce microplastics. See [reduce microplastics in drinking water](/plastiq-off/reduce-microplastics-in-drinking-water).
3. **Eat more fiber** — fiber supports regular elimination, one of the body's natural ways of moving material through the gut.
4. **Stay hydrated** — especially if you use any gut-level binder.
