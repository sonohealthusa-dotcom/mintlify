Clinoptilolite zeolite and food-grade diatomaceous earth (DE) are both natural mineral "binders," but they're chemically different and are used differently: **zeolite is a porous, negatively charged aluminosilicate** studied as a gut-level binder, while **DE is fossilized silica** more often used as an anti-caking agent and mechanical absorbent. Neither one "removes toxins" from your whole body — both act only in the digestive tract — so the honest way to choose is on sourcing, testing, and how well it fits your routine.

<Note>
Educational comparison. "Binders" support the body's own natural elimination in the gut; they are not treatments for microplastic exposure, mold illness, heavy-metal toxicity, or any disease. These statements have not been evaluated by the FDA. If you're worried about a real exposure, see a clinician.
</Note>

## What they are

| | Clinoptilolite zeolite | Food-grade diatomaceous earth |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Volcanic aluminosilicate mineral | Fossilized shells of diatoms (silica) |
| Structure | Porous "cage" with a negative surface charge | Porous silica skeletons |
| Main use | Gut-level binder / detox-pathway support | Anti-caking, mechanical absorbent, some binder use |
| Absorbed by the body? | Studied as non-absorbed; acts in the gut | Largely non-absorbed; acts in the gut |

## How each one works

Zeolite's negatively charged, honeycomb structure lets it act as an **ion-exchange binder** in the digestive tract, which is why clinoptilolite is the more-studied of the two for detox-pathway support. Diatomaceous earth works more by **surface area and mechanical absorption** — it's mostly amorphous silica.

Both work only where they physically are: in the gut. That's an important honesty point — a swallowed binder cannot reach and "pull" particles out of your organs or bloodstream.

## Safety at a glance

- **Zeolite (clinoptilolite):** generally well tolerated in studies; stay hydrated and space it 2 hours from medications. See [is zeolite safe?](/plastiq-off/is-zeolite-safe) and [the aluminum question](/plastiq-off/zeolite-aluminum-safety).
- **Diatomaceous earth:** use **food-grade only** — the *filter/pool-grade* form contains crystalline silica that is hazardous to inhale. Keep any powder away from your lungs.

## Common questions

**Is zeolite or diatomaceous earth better for microplastics?** Neither is proven to remove microplastics from your body. Zeolite is the more-studied gut-level binder; the biggest wins are still [reducing exposure](/plastiq-off/how-to-reduce-microplastics) and eating fiber.

**Can I take both together?** There's no strong reason to, and stacking binders can bind up nutrients and medications. If you use any binder, space it 2 hours from medications and check with your clinician.

**Which is gentler on the stomach?** Both can cause constipation if you're under-hydrated. Drink a full glass of water with either, and start low if your stomach is sensitive.
