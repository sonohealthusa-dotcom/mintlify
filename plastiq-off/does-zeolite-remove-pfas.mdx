## What PFAS are

PFAS (per- and polyfluoroalkyl substances) are a large family of synthetic "forever chemicals" used in non-stick, stain-resistant, and water-resistant products. They're called "forever chemicals" because they break down very slowly in the environment and in the body. They're a different concern from microplastics, though people often research them together.

## Why "a supplement removes PFAS" isn't supported

- **Binders act in the gut, not the bloodstream.** A gut-level binder like zeolite works on material passing through the digestive tract — it is not a magnet that pulls PFAS out of blood, liver, or kidney tissue.
- **Human evidence is lacking.** There's no reliable human research showing that zeolite or any dietary supplement meaningfully lowers PFAS body levels.
- **Real PFAS concerns need real evaluation.** PFAS exposure is assessed with blood testing and managed with a clinician — not with a detox supplement. See [do detox binders actually work?](/plastiq-off/do-detox-binders-work)

## What actually helps reduce PFAS exposure

1. **Know your water.** PFAS can occur in some drinking water; check local water-quality reports, and certified filters (such as reverse osmosis or activated-carbon systems rated for PFAS) can reduce levels. See [reduce microplastics in drinking water](/plastiq-off/reduce-microplastics-in-drinking-water) for the water-filtration mindset.
2. **Reduce product contact.** Limit non-stick cookware in poor condition and stain/water-resistant treatments where you can.
3. **Talk to your clinician** if you have a known high-exposure situation — they can order appropriate testing.
