## The terms, decoded

| Term | What it usually means | What to keep in mind |
|---|---|---|
| **Micronized zeolite** | Clinoptilolite milled into fine particles | Works as a gut-level binder; the best-studied use |
| **"Nano" zeolite** | Marketed as sub-micron particles | Claims of systemic (bloodstream) absorption are not well established in humans |
| **"Activated" zeolite** | Cleaned/processed for purity | Processing quality matters more than the label; ask for testing |
| **"Cellular" zeolite** | Marketing language, not a standard | No agreed scientific definition — judge by sourcing and testing, not the word |

## Does zeolite get absorbed into the body?

Human research on clinoptilolite generally describes it acting **in the gut**, where it can bind substances that are then eliminated normally — not being absorbed intact into the bloodstream to "hunt" toxins. That's why credible products use **structure/function** language ("supports natural detox pathways") rather than removal claims. For the evidence, see [do detox binders actually work?](/plastiq-off/do-detox-binders-work)

## Why "nano" claims deserve caution

Smaller isn't automatically better or safer. Claims that a "nano" or "cellular" zeolite is absorbed and pulls toxins from tissues go beyond what human studies support, and regulators have warned zeolite sellers about exactly these kinds of removal and disease claims. A well-milled, well-tested **micronized clinoptilolite** is a sensible, honest choice.
