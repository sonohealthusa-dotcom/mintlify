Zeolite and NAC/glutathione get lumped together as "detox," but they work in completely different places: **micronized clinoptilolite zeolite** is a **gut-level binder** that acts in the digestive tract, while **NAC and glutathione** are **antioxidant compounds the body uses inside cells**. They aren't interchangeable, and neither one "removes toxins" on its own.

## How each one works

**Clinoptilolite zeolite** has a porous, negatively charged structure that traps certain positively charged compounds inside its cavities in the digestive tract, so they can leave in stool rather than being reabsorbed. It works at the **gut level** and isn't meaningfully absorbed into the bloodstream — this is the binder in [Plastiq Off](/plastiq-off/overview). It's a *binder*, not a *chelator*; see [binder vs. chelator](/plastiq-off/binder-vs-chelator).

**NAC (N-acetylcysteine)** is an amino-acid compound the body can use to help make **glutathione**, an antioxidant your cells produce naturally. Rather than binding material in the gut, these support the body's **internal antioxidant** systems. Marketing sometimes calls both "detox," but they target very different steps — one in the gut, one inside your cells.

## Can you take them together?

There's no known harmful interaction between a gut-level zeolite binder and antioxidant supplements like NAC or glutathione — many people use them for different reasons. Because binders can affect absorption, space zeolite at least **2 hours** from other supplements and medications, and confirm any combination with your clinician or pharmacist, especially if you take prescription medication.
