The most effective way to reduce microplastics in your drinking water is to **filter tap water and store it in glass or stainless steel instead of plastic**. Research in 2024 found bottled water can contain hundreds of thousands of nanoplastic particles per liter — generally far more than typical tap water — so switching away from single-use plastic bottles is one of the highest-impact steps you can take.

## Is bottled water really worse than tap?

Often, yes. A 2024 study using advanced imaging found bottled water can carry hundreds of thousands of nanoplastic particles per liter, many shed from the plastic bottle and cap themselves. Tap water can contain microplastics too, but a good home filter typically reduces them, making filtered tap water in a non-plastic container a strong everyday choice.

## What actually helps

- **Filter your tap water.** Filters certified to reduce particulates (many carbon-block and reverse-osmosis systems) can lower microplastic content. Look for independent certification and replace cartridges on schedule.
- **Ditch single-use plastic bottles.** Refill a glass or stainless-steel bottle instead.
- **Store and serve water in glass or steel.** Avoid leaving plastic bottles in heat or sunlight, which increases particle shedding.
- **Don't heat water or drinks in plastic.** Heat drives more particles into liquids.
- **Boil-and-cool may help with hard water.** Some research suggests boiling hard tap water and filtering out the scale can trap a portion of microplastics, though results vary.
