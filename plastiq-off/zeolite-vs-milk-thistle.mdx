Zeolite and milk thistle both get called "detox" supplements, but they work in completely different places: **micronized clinoptilolite zeolite** is a **gut-level binder** that supports the body's natural elimination in the digestive tract, while **milk thistle (silymarin)** is an **herb traditionally used to support liver health**. They aren't interchangeable, and neither "removes toxins" on its own.

## How each one works

**Clinoptilolite zeolite** has a porous, negatively charged structure that traps certain positively charged compounds inside its cavities in the digestive tract, so they can leave in stool rather than being reabsorbed. It works at the **gut level** and isn't meaningfully absorbed into the bloodstream. This is the binder in [Plastiq Off](/plastiq-off/overview).

**Milk thistle** provides silymarin, a group of plant antioxidants studied for **liver support**. Unlike a binder, it's absorbed and acts systemically. It targets a different goal — liver-cell support — rather than binding material in the gut.

## Can you take them together?

Many people use a gut-level binder and a liver-support herb for different reasons, and they aren't the same tool. If you're considering combining supplements — especially if you take medications or have a health condition — confirm the plan with your clinician or pharmacist, and space any binder at least **2 hours** from medications and other supplements, since binders can affect absorption.
