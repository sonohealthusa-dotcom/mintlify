There's no supplement or drink that "flushes" microplastics out of your body. What actually helps is a combination: **reduce new exposure, eat more fiber, stay well hydrated, and support your gut** — because your body already has natural pathways for clearing particles, and the goal is to support them.

## Why people are asking

Microplastics have now been detected in human blood, lung, and other tissues, which has driven interest in lowering exposure — though what this means for health is still being studied ([see the evidence](/plastiq-off/are-microplastics-harmful)). Complete elimination isn't currently possible, so the practical focus is **exposure reduction plus supporting natural elimination**.

## The steps that actually help

### 1. Cut your biggest exposure sources

Plastic water bottles and food containers are among the largest daily sources. Switching to **glass or stainless steel** for water and food storage, and avoiding microwaving food in plastic, removes a big chunk of daily intake. Drinking water is a big one on its own — see [how to reduce microplastics in your drinking water](/plastiq-off/reduce-microplastics-in-drinking-water).

### 2. Eat more fiber

Soluble fibers (from apples, berries, onions, green bananas, oats) form a gel in the gut that can trap particles and compounds before they're absorbed, supporting elimination through stool.

### 3. Stay hydrated

Adequate water supports regular elimination — especially important if you use any binder, which can otherwise contribute to constipation.

### 4. Support your gut with a binder

Gut-level binders such as **clinoptilolite zeolite**, activated charcoal, and chlorella attach to certain compounds in the digestive tract. They're support tools, not cures, and human evidence for broad microplastic "detox" is still limited.

## Common questions

**Do bottled drinks contain more microplastics than tap water?**
Studies have found microplastic particles in many bottled beverages — water, soda, and other plastic-bottled drinks — largely from the packaging, and bottled tends to carry more than tap. Choosing glass or aluminum and filtering tap water both help.

**Does coffee from pods or K-cups add microplastics?**
Running very hot water through plastic pods or machine parts can release particles, per lab studies. A stainless-steel or glass brewer, a reusable metal filter, or paper filters reduce this.

**Are compostable or "bioplastic" products microplastic-free?**
Not necessarily — many still shed plastic-like particles and only fully break down under industrial composting. Reusable glass, steel, and ceramic remain the most reliable low-plastic swaps.
