Zeolite and bentonite clay are both natural mineral binders used to support the body's normal detoxification in the digestive tract, but they differ in structure and feel: **clinoptilolite zeolite** is a rigid, porous, negatively charged "cage" mineral, while **bentonite** is a soft, swelling clay. Neither one "removes toxins from your body" the way some marketing claims — both simply act as gut-level binders.

## Quick comparison

| Feature | Clinoptilolite zeolite | Bentonite clay |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Porous volcanic aluminosilicate mineral | Swelling clay (montmorillonite) |
| Structure | Rigid, cage-like, negatively charged | Expands and gels with water |
| Binding style | Relatively selective, gut-level | Broad, absorbent, gut-level |
| Typical format | Liquid drops, capsules, powder | Powder, capsules |
| Daily-use feel | Gentle; easy liquid dosing | Can be constipating if under-hydrated |
| Evidence in humans | Limited but studied | Limited |

## How each one works

Both are **gut-level binders** — they attach to certain compounds in the digestive tract so those leave in stool rather than being reabsorbed. Zeolite's crystal framework carries a negative charge that traps positively charged compounds in a fairly selective way. Bentonite is a swelling clay with a large surface area that absorbs broadly as it gels with water. Because both work in the gut and are not meaningfully absorbed into the bloodstream, credible products describe them as *supporting* natural detox pathways, not as a systemic or "cellular" detox.

## Tolerability and safety

Both are generally well tolerated, and both can cause constipation if you don't drink enough water. With any mineral binder, choose a **third-party-tested** product (natural clays and zeolites can carry trace lead and other metals), take it **at least 2 hours apart from medications**, and stay hydrated. Talk to your clinician if you're pregnant, nursing, on medication, or managing a health condition.

## Which should you choose?

- Want a **selective, gentle daily** binder in easy liquid form? Clinoptilolite zeolite fits well.
- Prefer a **traditional absorbent clay**? Bentonite, used with plenty of water.

There's no universal "best" — it comes down to format preference, tolerance, and product quality. Whatever you pick, verify third-party testing and keep it away from meals and medications.
