The four most common detox binders are **clinoptilolite zeolite, activated charcoal, chlorella, and bentonite clay** — and while all four work only in the digestive tract (none "flushes" toxins from your body), they differ in what they are, how gentle they are for daily use, and what else they offer. Here's a straight comparison to help you choose.

<Note>
Educational comparison, not medical advice. No binder supplement removes microplastics or heavy metals from your body. Binders are used to *support* your natural elimination pathways. If you have a specific exposure concern, see a clinician.
</Note>

## At a glance

| Binder | What it is | Best known for | Daily-use notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clinoptilolite zeolite** | A porous volcanic mineral (aluminosilicate) | Gentle, well-tolerated gut-level binding | Space from meds; hydrate; choose tested, US-made sources |
| **Activated charcoal** | Carbon processed to be highly porous | Broad, strong gut binding (used clinically in acute poisoning) | Non-selective — can bind nutrients and meds; can cause constipation; not ideal for casual daily use |
| **Chlorella** | A green freshwater algae | A whole-food source of chlorophyll and nutrients | More a nutrient-dense food than a targeted binder; quality/heavy-metal testing varies |
| **Bentonite clay** | A swelling volcanic clay | Traditional binder, often used in drinks/masks | Very absorbent; must hydrate well; grittier, and purity/heavy-metal testing matters a lot |
| **Diatomaceous earth** | Fossilized silica (diatom shells) | Mechanical/absorbent silica binder | Use **food-grade only** — never inhale powder (crystalline silica is a lung hazard); [full comparison](/plastiq-off/zeolite-vs-diatomaceous-earth) |

## How to think about the differences

- **Selectivity:** Zeolite and clay bind more selectively at the gut level, while activated charcoal binds broadly — which is powerful clinically but means it can also grab vitamins, minerals, and medications, so it's not well suited to everyday use.
- **Tolerability:** Clinoptilolite zeolite is generally well tolerated in human studies; the main practical issue for most binders is constipation if you don't drink enough water.
- **Purity:** Natural minerals (zeolite, bentonite) and algae (chlorella) vary by source, so third-party heavy-metal testing is essential.
- **Format:** Liquid, capsule, and powder each affect convenience more than effectiveness. A measured liquid makes a consistent daily dose easy.
