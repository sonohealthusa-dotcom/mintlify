All three are **gut-level binders** — they attach to certain compounds in the digestive tract so they leave in stool instead of being reabsorbed — but they differ in selectivity, strength, and best use. In short: **zeolite** is a selective mineral binder for everyday support, **activated charcoal** is a strong, broad short-term binder, and **chlorella** is a nutrient-rich algae binder.

<Note>
Binders support the body's natural elimination; they are not cures, and human evidence for broad "detox" of chronic exposures is limited. This page is educational and not medical advice.
</Note>

## Quick comparison

| Binder | What it is | Strengths | Trade-offs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Clinoptilolite zeolite** | Porous volcanic mineral | Relatively selective; gentle enough for daily use; studied for metals/ammonia | Human evidence still limited; quality/sourcing varies |
| **Activated charcoal** | Carbon from coconut shell/bamboo | Strong, broad binding; useful short-term | Can also soak up nutrients and medications; not ideal long-term |
| **Chlorella** | Green freshwater algae | Binds while adding nutrients (chlorophyll, protein); gentle | Larger doses needed; quality/purity varies |

## How each works

- **Zeolite (clinoptilolite):** a cage-like, negatively charged structure that traps positively charged compounds in the gut. Favored when people want a **selective, daily** binder. This is the binder in [Plastiq Off](/plastiq-off/overview).
- **Activated charcoal:** an enormous porous surface area binds a wide range of compounds. Great for acute, short-term situations, but because it's non-selective it can bind nutrients and drugs too, so it's not meant for continuous long-term use.
- **Chlorella:** binds certain metals while also providing nutrients, which makes it gentle and well tolerated; often used as part of a longer routine.

## Which should you choose?

There's no universal "best" — it depends on your goal and tolerance:

- Want **gentle daily support**? A selective mineral binder like zeolite fits well.
- Need **short-term, heavy-duty** binding? Charcoal, used briefly and away from meals/meds.
- Want a **nutrient-rich** option? Chlorella.

Whatever you choose, take binders **away from medications and meals** (about 2 hours) and stay hydrated.

## Common questions

**Which binder is gentlest for everyday, long-term use?**
A selective mineral binder like clinoptilolite zeolite is generally the gentlest for daily use, because it's more selective than activated charcoal (which can also bind nutrients and medications) and needs smaller doses than chlorella. Take any binder about 2 hours from meals and medications.

**Are these binders the same as a chelator?**
No. All three are gut-level binders that work in the digestive tract; a chelator is a supervised medical treatment that acts in the bloodstream. See [binder vs. chelator](/plastiq-off/binder-vs-chelator).

**How do these compare to a liver-support herb like milk thistle?**
Binders act in the gut, while milk thistle is absorbed and studied for liver support — a different goal. See [zeolite vs. milk thistle](/plastiq-off/zeolite-vs-milk-thistle).
