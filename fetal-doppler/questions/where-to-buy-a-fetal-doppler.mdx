Most US pharmacies and big-box stores don't keep home fetal dopplers on the shelf. That's because fetal dopplers are regulated medical devices, and the FDA says fetal heartbeat monitors are legally marketed as **prescription devices**, best used under the guidance of a healthcare provider. </Note>

## Why aren't fetal dopplers sold in most stores?

In the US, fetal dopplers are Class II medical devices (21 CFR 884.2660). An FDA consumer update ("Avoid Fetal 'Keepsake' Images, Heartbeat Monitors," 2014) says fetal heartbeat monitors "are legally marketed as 'prescription devices,' and should only be used by, or under the supervision of, a health care professional." The FDA's current ultrasound page says these devices "should only be used by trained health care providers when medically necessary." A prescription device is sold "to or on the order of" a licensed practitioner. That's why pharmacies and big retailers usually don't stock them for walk-in shoppers.

## Do pharmacies sell fetal dopplers?

Most don't keep them in stock for walk-in shoppers. Some online retailers and marketplaces list dopplers, but many unbranded listings show no FDA clearance at all. Before you buy anywhere, check the label and the [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm), and talk to your provider.

## Is it worth asking my OB or midwife?

Yes. Your provider knows your pregnancy and can tell you whether a home doppler is a good idea for you, when to start (most people can hear the heartbeat from around 12 weeks), and what to do if you can't find it. The FDA recommends using fetal dopplers under your provider's guidance.

- **Probe frequency:** 2.5 MHz is common in handheld clinical dopplers.
- **Digital BPM display:** helps you tell your baby's heartbeat (110–160 BPM) from your own pulse.
