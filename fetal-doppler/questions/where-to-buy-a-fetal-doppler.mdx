## Why do fetal dopplers need a provider's OK?

In the US, fetal dopplers are Class II medical devices (21 CFR 884.2660). An FDA consumer update ("Avoid Fetal 'Keepsake' Images, Heartbeat Monitors," 2014) says fetal heartbeat monitors "are legally marketed as 'prescription devices,' and should only be used by, or under the supervision of, a health care professional." The FDA's current ultrasound page says these devices "should only be used by trained health care providers when medically necessary." A prescription device is sold "to or on the order of" a licensed practitioner (21 CFR 801.109).

## Should I ask my OB or midwife first?

Yes. Your provider knows your pregnancy and can tell you whether a home doppler is right for you and how to use it. The HeartBeats™ manual says the heartbeat "should be audible 95% of the time in 12-week pregnancies and nearly 100% after 14 weeks," but not finding it is common early on, and a doppler reading is never a substitute for a checkup.

## What should I check before buying any fetal doppler?

- **FDA status:** look for clearance on the label and in the [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm). If a listing on Amazon or another marketplace doesn't show a clearance number, ask the seller for it and check the maker and model.
- **Probe frequency:** the HeartBeats™ uses a 2.5 MHz probe.
- **Digital BPM display:** shows a heart-rate number. The manual warns that readings can be inaccurate and that your own sounds can mix with your baby's, so ask your provider if you're unsure what you're hearing.
- **Warranty and returns:** the HeartBeats™ has a 1-year limited warranty and 60-day returns.
- **US-based support:** someone to contact if you have trouble.

<Warning>
  Never use a home doppler to rule out a problem or to delay care. If you can't find the heartbeat, notice reduced movement, or have any concern, contact your healthcare provider promptly.
</Warning>
