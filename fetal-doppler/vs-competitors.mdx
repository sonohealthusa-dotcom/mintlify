## Key Differentiators: HeartBeats™

### FDA Clearance: Check Every Device

FDA clearance means a device was reviewed as substantially equivalent to a legally marketed device, its labeling includes safety warnings, and its maker is subject to FDA adverse-event reporting. Some well-known home dopplers are cleared (see the table). Some devices sold on Amazon and other marketplaces don't show a clearance number, so check the label and the maker. You can look up any device in the [FDA 510(k) database](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/scripts/cdrh/cfdocs/cfpmn/pmn.cfm).

### 2.5 MHz Probe Frequency

HeartBeats™ uses a **2.5 MHz probe**, a frequency common in handheld clinical dopplers. Many home competitors use 3 MHz probes, which penetrate slightly less deeply.

In the early second trimester both frequencies work. Lower frequencies reach a little deeper in general; whether that helps you is a question for your provider.

### What to Watch for With the Cheapest Options

Before buying a very low-cost doppler on Amazon or another marketplace, check:
- Whether the label shows an FDA clearance you can find in the FDA database
- Who makes it, and whether the instructions are clear for home use
- What warranty and return support the seller offers

## Talk to Your Provider

The FDA says fetal heartbeat monitors "are legally marketed as 'prescription devices,' and should only be used by, or under the supervision of, a health care professional." Ask your OB or midwife before you start, and never use a doppler in place of prenatal care.
