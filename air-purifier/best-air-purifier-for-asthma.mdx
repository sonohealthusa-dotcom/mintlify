## Why filtration grade matters for asthma

Asthma flare-ups are often triggered by microscopic particles: dust-mite debris, pet dander, mold spores, pollen fragments, and fine smoke. Standard "True HEPA" (HEPA 13) captures 99.95% of particles at 0.3 microns; **HEPA 14 captures 99.995%** — about 10x fewer particles slipping through. Over hours of continuous use, that difference means fewer airborne triggers reaching sensitive airways. See [HEPA 14 Filtration](/air-purifier/hepa-14-filtration).

## Avoid ozone: it can make asthma worse

This is the single most important safety point for asthma. **Ozone is a lung irritant that can worsen asthma**, and some ionizing or "ozone-generating" purifiers emit it. The AirPro uses **mechanical HEPA 14, activated carbon, and fully enclosed UV-C only — no ionizer and no ozone.** If you have asthma, avoid ozone generators entirely.

<Warning>
Do not use ozone-generating air purifiers if you have asthma. The California Air Resources Board lists ozone as a respiratory irritant that can worsen asthma and reduce lung function. Choose ozone-free mechanical filtration.
</Warning>

## Does the evidence support air purifiers for asthma?

Research and public-health agencies find that HEPA filtration can reduce indoor particulate and allergen levels and may ease respiratory symptoms — but it works best **alongside** removing pollutant sources, controlling humidity, and following your asthma action plan. A purifier is one part of a management plan, not a cure or a replacement for medication.

<Note>
An air purifier reduces airborne triggers; it does not diagnose, treat, or cure asthma. Keep taking prescribed controller and rescue medication and follow your clinician's asthma action plan. The [EPA Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home) explains how filtration fits alongside source control and ventilation.
</Note>
