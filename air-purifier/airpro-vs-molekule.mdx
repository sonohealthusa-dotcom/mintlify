The core difference is the technology and how well it's proven: the SonoHealth AirPro uses **medical-grade HEPA 14 (99.995% at 0.3 microns) plus enclosed UV-C**, a mechanical filter validated to the EN 1822 standard, while Molekule is built around **PECO (photoelectrochemical oxidation)**, a chemistry-based approach that independent reviewers found slower and less consistent at removing airborne particles than mechanical HEPA.

## What is the actual difference in technology?

The AirPro traps particles physically. Air passes through a pre-filter, a HEPA 14 filter that captures 99.995% of particles at 0.3 microns (and 99.9% down to 0.1 microns), and an activated-carbon stage for odors and VOCs, with a fully enclosed UV-C lamp for germicidal treatment.

Molekule's signature **PECO** technology instead coats a filter with a catalyst and shines light on it to break down pollutants chemically. It's an interesting idea, but it is a fundamentally different mechanism from standards-tested mechanical filtration — and it is what most of the independent criticism has focused on.

## Which one actually removes more particles?

For capturing pollen, dust, dander, mold spores, and fine smoke, mechanical HEPA is the proven method. Independent product testing has repeatedly found that **PECO-only devices removed airborne particles more slowly** than conventional True HEPA and HEPA-grade purifiers, which is why later Molekule designs added a mechanical filter of their own.

The AirPro doesn't rely on a novel mechanism: HEPA 14 is the **highest common consumer HEPA grade**, one step above the True HEPA (H13, 99.97%) filters most purifiers use, and it is defined and measured by the EN 1822 standard. See [HEPA 14 Filtration](/air-purifier/hepa-14-filtration) and [HEPA 14 vs. True HEPA](/air-purifier/hepa-14-vs-true-hepa).
