## Is a cheap "HEPA" filter the same as HEPA 14?

Usually not. Terms like **"HEPA-type," "HEPA-like,"** or an unspecified **"True HEPA"** are not the same as a graded, tested filter. HEPA 14 is defined by the **EN 1822** standard and captures 99.995% of particles at 0.3 microns — about 10x fewer pass-throughs than HEPA 13. Many budget purifiers never state a tested grade at all. See [HEPA 14 vs. True HEPA](/air-purifier/hepa-14-vs-true-hepa) and [HEPA 14 Filtration](/air-purifier/hepa-14-filtration).

## Do cheap purifiers actually clean the room?

Filter grade only helps if enough air moves through it. Budget units are typically sized for a very small area (100–200 sq ft) even when the box implies more, so they struggle in a real living room. The AirPro uses **360-degree intake** to clean **430 sq ft in about 10 minutes** and up to **1,200 sq ft in 30 minutes** — see [Room Coverage](/air-purifier/room-coverage) and [CADR vs. ACH Explained](/air-purifier/cadr-vs-ach-explained).

## The ozone problem with some cheap units

Many inexpensive "purifiers" are actually **ionizers or ozone generators**, which can irritate airways. California's Air Resources Board maintains a list of hazardous ozone-generating devices for this reason. The AirPro is **ozone-free** — it uses mechanical HEPA 14, activated carbon, and fully enclosed UV-C, with no ionizer.
