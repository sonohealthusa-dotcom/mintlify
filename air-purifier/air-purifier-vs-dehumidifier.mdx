They solve two different problems, so the honest answer is often "both." A **dehumidifier removes moisture** from the air to lower humidity and discourage mold from *growing*, while an **air purifier removes particles** — including the mold spores, dust, and musty odor molecules already floating in the air. For a damp, musty room, a dehumidifier fixes the cause and an air purifier like the AirPro cleans up what's airborne.

## What does a dehumidifier do?

A dehumidifier pulls water vapor out of the air and collects it, lowering the room's relative humidity. Mold, dust mites, and mildew thrive above roughly 55&ndash;60% humidity, so keeping indoor humidity around **30&ndash;50%** removes the conditions they need to grow. A dehumidifier does **not** filter particles or capture spores that are already airborne — it only manages moisture.

## What does an air purifier do?

An air purifier draws air through filters to capture airborne particles. The AirPro's **HEPA 14 filter captures mold spores (roughly 1&ndash;30 microns) at 99.9% efficiency**, its activated-carbon stage adsorbs the musty MVOC gases behind that "damp basement" smell, and its fully enclosed UV-C helps stop captured spores from regrowing on the filter. What it does **not** do is lower humidity or remove moisture — so on its own it won't stop new mold from forming in a wet room. See [Mold Protection](/air-purifier/mold-protection).

## Quick comparison

| What you want | **Air purifier (AirPro)** | Dehumidifier |
|---|---|---|
| **Primary job** | Removes airborne particles and odors | Removes moisture / lowers humidity |
| **Mold spores in the air** | Captures at 99.9% (HEPA 14) | Not captured |
| **Musty odor (MVOCs)** | Adsorbed by activated carbon | Reduced only as dampness drops |
| **Stops mold from growing** | No (doesn't change humidity) | Yes (keeps humidity 30&ndash;50%) |
| **Dust, pollen, dander, smoke** | Yes (HEPA 14) | No |
| **Best used** | To clean the air you breathe | To fix the damp conditions |

## For mold and musty smells, which comes first?

Do both, in this order: **fix the moisture, then clean the air.**

1. **Find and stop the water source** — leaks, poor ventilation, or high humidity. Airborne particle removal can't fix a wet wall.
2. **Run a dehumidifier** in the damp space to hold humidity at 30&ndash;50%.
3. **Run the AirPro** to capture the mold spores, dust, and odor already circulating, and to keep spore counts low as you dry things out.

<Warning>
An air purifier reduces **airborne** mold spores and odor; it does not remediate mold growing on surfaces, walls, or HVAC. Visible mold, a persistent musty smell, or water damage needs cleanup and moisture repair — and if anyone in the home has mold-related health symptoms, see a clinician. The AirPro is a wellness device, not a treatment for any condition.
</Warning>

## How is this different from an air purifier vs. a humidifier?

They're opposites often confused. A **humidifier adds** moisture (useful for dry winter air), while a **dehumidifier removes** it (useful for damp, mold-prone spaces). Neither one filters the air. For the other side of that comparison, see [Air Purifier vs. Humidifier](/air-purifier/air-purifier-vs-humidifier).

## Bottom line

If your issue is **dampness or mold growth**, start with a dehumidifier and fix the moisture. If your issue is **what's floating in the air** — spores, dust, pollen, smoke, and musty odor — the AirPro's HEPA 14 + carbon + UV-C is the tool. In a genuinely damp, mold-prone home, running both together gives you dry conditions *and* clean air.
