## Why are basements harder to keep clean?

Basements sit below grade, so they tend to be **more humid, less ventilated, and cooler** — ideal conditions for mold spores and that classic "musty" smell (from microbial VOCs). They also collect settled dust, and some have radon seeping in from the soil. A purifier addresses the **airborne** part: fine spores, dust, and odors floating in the air. It cannot lower humidity, remove radon, or clean surfaces — which is why it's one tool in a basement plan, not the whole plan.

## Do I need a dehumidifier too?

Usually, yes — and it's the most important pairing. A purifier removes airborne particles and odors, but **only a dehumidifier lowers the humidity that lets mold grow** in the first place. The EPA recommends keeping indoor humidity below about 50% to control mold and dust mites. Run a dehumidifier to dry the space and the AirPro to clear airborne spores and musty odor. See [air purifier vs. dehumidifier](/air-purifier/air-purifier-vs-dehumidifier) for how they divide the work.

## Will it get rid of the musty basement smell?

It helps. The AirPro's **activated carbon** adsorbs many of the musty microbial VOCs behind that smell, while the HEPA 14 filter removes the airborne spores that shed it. But if the source is ongoing dampness or hidden mold, the smell will return until you fix the moisture — the purifier is harm reduction for the air, not a cure for the cause. More in [mold and black mold protection](/air-purifier/mold-protection).

## Can an air purifier remove radon from a basement?

No. Radon is a **gas** that seeps from soil, and neither HEPA nor carbon reliably removes it. If radon is a concern, test with a radon kit and use a radon mitigation system — an air purifier is not a substitute. This is a safety limit worth knowing before you rely on a purifier for basement air.
