The best air purifier for a classroom covers the full room, runs quietly enough not to distract students, and is ozone-free for all-day use around children. Medical-grade HEPA filtration matters most for capturing dust, pollen, and airborne particles in a busy shared space. The SonoHealth AirPro HEPA 14 meets each of these.

## Why does air quality matter in classrooms?

Classrooms pack many people into one room, which raises airborne dust, allergens, and respiratory particles. The EPA notes that improving ventilation and adding portable air cleaners with HEPA filters can help reduce indoor airborne particles in schools. Cleaner air is also linked to better comfort and focus.

<Note>
An air purifier is a wellness appliance, not a medical device or a substitute for a school's ventilation, cleaning, or public-health measures. It reduces airborne particles but does not treat or prevent any illness.
</Note>

## What should a school look for in a classroom air purifier?

- **Whole-room coverage.** Match coverage to the room. The AirPro cleans **430 sq ft in about 10 minutes** and up to **1,200 sq ft in 30 minutes** with 360-degree intake — enough for most standard classrooms. See [Room Coverage](/air-purifier/room-coverage).
- **Quiet operation.** A loud unit gets turned off. The AirPro runs **25–50 dB** (25 dB Night Mode), quiet enough not to compete with a teacher's voice.
- **Ozone-free.** Avoid ionizing or ozone-generating devices around children. The AirPro uses mechanical HEPA 14, activated carbon, and **fully enclosed UV-C** with no ionizer and no ozone. SonoHealth is checking the AirPro's CARB certification status; contact support for the current status.
- **Medical-grade filtration.** HEPA 14 (99.995% at 0.3 microns, EN 1822) captures pollen, dust, chalk and paper dust, mold spores, and fine PM2.5. See [HEPA 14 Filtration](/air-purifier/hepa-14-filtration).
- **Low upkeep.** Filters last **6–12 months**, with a built-in tracker that signals replacement — practical for a busy school year.

## How many units does a classroom need?

Match total coverage to room size and ceiling height. One AirPro suits a standard classroom; larger rooms, portables with high ceilings, or open-plan spaces may need two units placed apart for even air movement. Give each unit a few inches of clearance for its 360-degree intake — see [Where to Place an Air Purifier](/air-purifier/where-to-place-an-air-purifier).
