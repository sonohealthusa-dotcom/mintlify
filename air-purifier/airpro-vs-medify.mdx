## Which one filters more thoroughly?

Both are genuine HEPA purifiers, but they sit one grade apart. The Medify MA-40 uses an **H13-class filter** marketed as capturing 99.9% of particles at 0.3 microns — the "True HEPA" tier. The AirPro's **HEPA 14** filter is certified to **99.995% at 0.3 microns** under EN 1822, so roughly **10&times; fewer particles** slip through at the most-penetrating particle size. For everyday dust the gap is small; for wildfire smoke, ultrafine allergen fragments, or an immunocompromised household it is the meaningful upgrade. See [HEPA 14 vs. True HEPA](/air-purifier/hepa-14-vs-true-hepa).

## Does UV-C matter here?

This is the clearest functional gap. The AirPro adds a **fully enclosed UV-C (254 nm)** stage that targets bacteria and mold after the filter traps them, sealed inside the unit with no user exposure and **no ozone**. The MA-40 relies on filtration alone. Learn how the AirPro's works in [UV-C Sterilization](/air-purifier/uv-c-sterilization).

## What about coverage and noise?

Medify publishes coverage using air-change rates, while SonoHealth publishes coverage as **area over time** (430 sq ft in 10 minutes; up to 1,200 sq ft in 30 minutes). On noise, the AirPro's 25 dB Night Mode is engineered for bedrooms and nurseries.
