An air conditioner and an air purifier do different jobs, so they are not interchangeable. An **air conditioner cools and dehumidifies** the air, while an **air purifier filters out particles** like dust, pollen, smoke, mold spores, and pet dander. If your goal is cleaner air for allergies or asthma, you need a purifier — a cooling AC alone won't do it.

## What does an air conditioner actually filter?

Most air conditioners have a coarse filter, but it exists mainly to protect the unit's coils from large debris, not to clean the air you breathe. These filters are low on the MERV scale and capture very little fine particulate. A window or portable AC recirculates and cools air; a central AC filters only when the system is actively running and only to its (usually modest) filter grade.

## What does an air purifier do that an AC doesn't?

A dedicated air purifier draws room air through fine media to capture airborne particles continuously, regardless of temperature. The AirPro's **HEPA 14 filter captures 99.995% of particles at 0.3 microns and 99.9% down to 0.1 microns**, plus an activated-carbon stage for smoke, VOCs, and odors and a fully enclosed, ozone-free UV-C stage. An AC does none of this at that level.

## Do I need both an air purifier and an air conditioner?

Often, yes — they complement each other. Run the AC to stay cool and comfortable, and run a HEPA air purifier to keep fine particles and allergens down. During wildfire smoke or high-pollen periods, keep windows closed, run the AC on **recirculate**, and let the purifier clean the air. See [Wildfire Smoke Guide](/air-purifier/wildfire-smoke-guide).

<Note>
An air purifier reduces airborne triggers but is a wellness device, not a medical treatment for allergies, asthma, or any condition. The EPA recommends combining filtration with source control and ventilation. See the [EPA Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home).
</Note>

## What about humidity?

An AC removes some moisture as it cools, but for a persistently damp, musty space you may need a dedicated dehumidifier. An air purifier does not change humidity — it only filters particles. For the full breakdown, see [Air Purifier vs. Dehumidifier](/air-purifier/air-purifier-vs-dehumidifier).
