Yes, most homes still benefit from a portable air purifier even with a good furnace filter. An **HVAC furnace filter** cleans air only when the system's fan is running and is limited to its MERV grade, while a **portable HEPA 14 purifier** filters the air in your occupied room continuously and to a far higher standard. They work best together.

## What does a furnace (HVAC) filter do?

A furnace filter sits in your central heating and cooling system, capturing particles as air passes through — but only while the blower is running. Its job is partly to protect the equipment, and residential systems are usually limited to around MERV 8–13 because higher-MERV filters restrict airflow and can strain the blower. So whole-home filtration is real but intermittent and capped at a MERV grade.

## How does that compare to a HEPA 14 air purifier?

A standalone purifier runs on its own schedule in the room you're actually in, independent of the HVAC. The AirPro uses **HEPA 14 (99.995% at 0.3 microns, 99.9% at 0.1 microns)** — far beyond the MERV scale, which tops out at MERV 16 (roughly 95% at 0.3 microns). That means a portable HEPA 14 unit captures ultrafine particles a furnace filter lets through, and it keeps working even when the furnace or AC is off.

| Feature | **HVAC furnace filter** | **Portable HEPA 14 purifier (AirPro)** |
|---|---|---|
| **Efficiency** | MERV 8–13 (up to MERV 16) | HEPA 14 (99.995% at 0.3 microns) |

| **Where it cleans** | Whole home (when running) | The specific room you're in |

| **Odors and VOCs** | No | Yes (activated carbon) |
| **Main purpose** | Protect equipment + broad filtration | Targeted clean air where you breathe |

## Should I upgrade my furnace filter or buy an air purifier?

Do both where you can. Use the **highest-MERV furnace filter your system supports** (check the manual so you don't restrict airflow) for whole-home baseline filtration, and add a **portable HEPA 14 purifier** in the bedroom or main living area for continuous, higher-grade filtration where it matters most. Running the HVAC fan on "circulate" plus a room purifier gives the most consistent results.

<Note>
An air purifier reduces airborne particles but is a wellness device, not a medical treatment. Filtration works best alongside removing pollutant sources and ventilating with clean outdoor air. See the [EPA Guide to Air Cleaners in the Home](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/guide-air-cleaners-home).
</Note>

## Can I put a HEPA 14 filter in my furnace instead?

Generally no. True HEPA 14 media is too dense for most residential HVAC blowers and would choke airflow, which is why whole-home systems use MERV-rated filters and standalone rooms use HEPA. See [HEPA 14 vs. True HEPA](/air-purifier/hepa-14-vs-true-hepa) for how the grades compare.
