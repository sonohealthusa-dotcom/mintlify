Both are serious filtration purifiers; the difference is price and positioning. IQAir is a premium Swiss brand whose HealthPro line uses **HyperHEPA** filtration and is individually tested, but it typically costs several times more. The SonoHealth AirPro delivers **medical-grade HEPA 14 (99.995% at 0.3 microns, EN 1822) plus enclosed UV-C** with a 60-day trial.

## How do the filters compare on grade?

IQAir's HyperHEPA is a genuine high-efficiency mechanical filter that the company states captures very fine particles, and each unit ships with an individual test certificate — a real strength. The AirPro uses **HEPA 14**, the highest common consumer HEPA grade defined by the EN 1822 standard, capturing 99.995% at 0.3 microns and 99.9% down to 0.1 microns. Both are proven mechanical approaches; see [HEPA 14 Filtration](/air-purifier/hepa-14-filtration).

## What about UV-C and odors?

The AirPro pairs its HEPA 14 with an **activated-carbon stage** for smoke, VOCs, and cooking odors and a **fully enclosed UV-C (254 nm)** germicidal stage — see [UV-C Sterilization](/air-purifier/uv-c-sterilization). IQAir offers carbon/VOC options on some models but does not use a UV-C stage; its focus is filtration alone.

## Coverage and noise

The AirPro clears about **430 sq ft in 10 minutes** and up to **1,200 sq ft in 30 minutes**, running at 25&ndash;50 dB (25 dB Night Mode). IQAir's flagship units are physically large, powerful, and rated for big rooms, but they are heavier and generally louder at higher speeds. For most bedrooms, nurseries, and living rooms, the AirPro's quiet 25 dB night operation is a practical advantage — see [Room Coverage](/air-purifier/room-coverage).
