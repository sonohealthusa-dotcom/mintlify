The main difference is how each filter is graded: the SonoHealth AirPro uses **medical-grade HEPA 14 defined and measured by the EN 1822 standard (99.995% at 0.3 microns)**, while AirDoctor markets a proprietary **"UltraHEPA"** filter that is not a standardized HEPA grade. Both target fine particles and VOCs; the AirPro adds an enclosed UV-C stage and a 60-day trial.

## What is "UltraHEPA" and how is it different from HEPA 14?

"UltraHEPA" is AirDoctor's own brand name, not an independent classification. AirDoctor advertises capture of very small particles, but the term is not tied to the EN 1822 scale the way HEPA 13 (True HEPA) and HEPA 14 are. The AirPro's **HEPA 14** is the highest common consumer HEPA grade — one step above True HEPA — and is defined by a published standard you can verify. See [HEPA 14 Filtration](/air-purifier/hepa-14-filtration) and [HEPA 14 vs. True HEPA](/air-purifier/hepa-14-vs-true-hepa).

## Which removes more particles?

For everyday pollutants — pollen, dust, pet dander, mold spores, and fine smoke — both rely on dense mechanical media, which is the proven method. The meaningful, checkable claim is the grade: the AirPro captures **99.995% of particles at 0.3 microns and 99.9% down to 0.1 microns** to the EN 1822 standard. AirDoctor's headline figures rest on its own testing rather than a standardized grade, so the AirPro is the more transparent, verifiable spec.

## How do they compare on odors, VOCs, and germs?

Both include an activated-carbon/VOC stage for smoke, cooking odors, and formaldehyde. The AirPro adds a **fully enclosed UV-C (254 nm)** germicidal stage that acts on bacteria and mold *after* the HEPA 14 filter traps them, with no ozone and no user UV exposure — see [UV-C Sterilization](/air-purifier/uv-c-sterilization). AirDoctor relies on its dual filter without a dedicated UV-C stage.
