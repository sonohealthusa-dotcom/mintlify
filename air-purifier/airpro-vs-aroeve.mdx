## Which filters more thoroughly?

This is the core difference. Aroeve markets "H13" or "HEPA" filtration; when a grade is stated it is usually the **H13 ("True HEPA") class, about 99.95–99.97% at 0.3 microns**, and many budget listings do not publish an independent **EN 1822** certification at all. The AirPro's **HEPA 14** is certified to **99.995% at 0.3 microns**, so roughly **10× fewer particles** slip through at the hardest-to-trap size, and it captures 99.9% down to 0.1 microns. See [HEPA 14 vs. True HEPA](/air-purifier/hepa-14-vs-true-hepa) and [what "HEPA-type" really means](/air-purifier/do-air-purifiers-work).

For light, general dust in a small room, an Aroeve is genuinely serviceable. If you are buying for **allergies, asthma, wildfire smoke, mold, or an immunocompromised household member**, the verified higher grade is the meaningful upgrade.

## Does UV-C matter here?

It is a clear functional gap. The AirPro adds a **fully enclosed UV-C (254 nm)** stage that targets bacteria and mold captured on the filter; standard Aroeve models have **no UV-C**. The AirPro's UV-C is sealed inside the unit, so there is no user exposure and **no ozone** — how it works is covered in [UV-C sterilization](/air-purifier/uv-c-sterilization). This matters most in damp or [mold-prone spaces](/air-purifier/best-air-purifier-for-mold).

## What about coverage and value?

Aroeve wins on up-front price, full stop. The trade-off is coverage and longevity: budget units use smaller fans, so their **highest advertised square footage is usually quoted at a low air-change rate** — real, frequent cleaning happens in a smaller area. The AirPro is rated to clear **430 sq ft in about 10 minutes** and up to **1,200 sq ft in 30 minutes** with 360-degree intake; match either brand to your room with [room coverage](/air-purifier/room-coverage) and [CADR vs. ACH explained](/air-purifier/cadr-vs-ach-explained). The AirPro also adds a **2-year warranty (lifetime with a filter subscription)** and a **60-day free trial**.

- **Choose an Aroeve** if you want the lowest possible price for light dust and pollen in a small room and don't need a certified grade or UV-C.
