## What are VOCs, and where do they come from?

Volatile organic compounds (VOCs) are gases released from everyday sources: new furniture, carpets, paint, adhesives, cleaning products, air fresheners, and building materials — the "new" smell after a renovation is largely off-gassing VOCs. Common examples include formaldehyde, benzene, and toluene. VOCs can cause headaches, eye and throat irritation, and worsen asthma or chemical sensitivity.

## Why HEPA alone can't remove VOCs

HEPA filters — even HEPA 14 — capture **particles**, not **gases**. VOCs are molecular gases far smaller than any particle a HEPA filter traps. Removing them requires **adsorption** onto activated carbon, where the gas molecules stick to the carbon's vast internal surface area. That's why a purifier for VOCs must have a genuine carbon stage, not just a HEPA filter.

## Avoid "ozone" and "PCO" shortcuts

Some purifiers claim to "destroy" VOCs with ozone or photocatalytic oxidation (PCO). **Ozone is a lung irritant** and can itself create harmful byproducts. The AirPro is **ozone-free** — it uses mechanical filtration and carbon adsorption plus fully enclosed UV-C, with no ionizer. For chemical sensitivity, ozone-free is the safer choice.

<Warning>
Air purifiers reduce airborne VOCs but do not eliminate the source. For high VOC levels — during active renovation, new flooring, or paint curing — also ventilate with fresh air and let materials off-gas. An air purifier is a supplement to source control and ventilation, not a replacement.
</Warning>
