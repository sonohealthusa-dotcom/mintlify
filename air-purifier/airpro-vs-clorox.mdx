## Filtration: HEPA 14 vs. Clorox's True HEPA

Clorox air purifiers use a **True HEPA (H13-class)** filter — capturing roughly 99.97% of particles at 0.3 microns — paired with activated carbon for odors. The AirPro sits one grade higher at **HEPA 14 (99.995% at 0.3 microns, EN 1822)**, letting through about **10&times; fewer particles** at the most-penetrating particle size, and adds a **fully enclosed UV-C** stage Clorox models don't include. See [HEPA 14 Filtration](/air-purifier/hepa-14-filtration).

## Where Clorox wins

Clorox is a trusted household name with wide retail availability, and some models include an **air-quality sensor and auto mode** that adjusts fan speed automatically — a convenience the AirPro doesn't offer (the AirPro uses manual speeds, a 2/4/6-hour Smart Timer, and a 25 dB Night Mode, and many owners simply run it continuously). If auto mode matters most to you and True HEPA is sufficient, Clorox is a fair choice.

## Where the AirPro wins

The AirPro leads on **filtration grade (HEPA 14)**, adds **enclosed ozone-free UV-C**, covers up to **1,200 sq ft**, runs as low as **25 dB** in Night Mode, and includes a **60-day free trial** and a **2-year warranty** (lifetime with the [filter subscription](/air-purifier/filter-replacement)). Facts are grounded in the [AirPro product page](https://sonohealth.com/products/air-pro-hepa-filter-air-purifier).
