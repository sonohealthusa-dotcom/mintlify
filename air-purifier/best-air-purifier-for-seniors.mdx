The best air purifier for a senior is ozone-free, quiet enough to run overnight, simple to operate, and strong enough to clean the whole room. Older adults are more sensitive to fine particles, so medical-grade HEPA filtration without an ozone-emitting ionizer matters most. The SonoHealth AirPro HEPA 14 meets each of these.

## Why does air quality matter more for older adults?

As we age, lungs and the immune system become more sensitive to airborne pollutants. The EPA notes that older adults, along with people who have asthma, COPD, or heart disease, are among the groups most affected by fine particle pollution. Reducing indoor particles like dust, smoke, pollen, and mold spores can lower day-to-day exposure at home.

<Note>
An air purifier is a wellness appliance, not a medical treatment. It reduces airborne particles but does not treat COPD, asthma, heart disease, or any condition, and it is not a substitute for prescribed medication or home oxygen. Anyone managing a health condition should follow their clinician's guidance.
</Note>

## What should seniors look for in an air purifier?

- **Ozone-free operation.** Avoid ionizing or "ozone-generating" purifiers, which can irritate sensitive airways. The AirPro uses mechanical HEPA 14 filtration, activated carbon, and fully enclosed UV-C with **no ionizer and no ozone**.
- **Quiet enough for sleep.** A loud unit gets switched off. The AirPro runs at **25 dB in Night Mode** (quieter than a whisper) so it can run continuously.
- **Simple controls.** No app or Wi-Fi setup required — the AirPro is controlled with buttons on the unit and a 2/4/6-hour timer.
- **Whole-room coverage.** Match coverage to the room. The AirPro cleans **430 sq ft in about 10 minutes** and up to **1,200 sq ft in 30 minutes**.
- **Low upkeep.** Filters last **6–12 months**, with a built-in tracker that signals when to replace them.

## Is the AirPro HSA or FSA eligible for a senior on a fixed income?

Often yes, but usually only with a **Letter of Medical Necessity** from a provider, because the IRS treats air purifiers as dual-purpose items. Check current rules in [IRS Publication 502](https://www.irs.gov/publications/p502) and with your plan administrator.

## Is an air purifier enough on its own?

No — it works best alongside source control and ventilation. The EPA recommends reducing pollutant sources and ventilating with clean outdoor air in addition to filtration. Keep indoor humidity at 30–50% to limit mold and dust mites, and avoid indoor smoking.

This is general information, not medical advice. If an older adult has worsening breathing, chest symptoms, or a chronic lung or heart condition, contact a clinician.
